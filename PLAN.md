# Home Library — Plan

A private catalog of one household's books, and later the articles and handouts kept from school, used from a desktop browser and from a phone. It lives at **books.jamescwill.com**, with **books.mathdad.me** as a full alias: either name serves the whole site and stays on that name.

This file holds the product decisions, the data model, and the phase order. Anything that changes them changes this file in the same change, with a dated line in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md).

## What it does

- Catalogs every book with as much bibliographic detail as public sources give: title, subtitle, contributors and their roles, series and volume, publisher, place, date, edition, format, pages, dimensions, language, Dewey, Library of Congress classification, subjects, description, cover.
- Adds a book by ISBN (typed or scanned with the phone's camera), by a title search, or by hand.
- Fetches a cover, or takes the owner's own photograph of it.
- Records each physical copy: new or used, where and when it came from and for how much, condition, dust jacket, signed, where it sits.
- Tracks reading: Unread, Reading (with the page reached), Read; dates and a rating; every earlier read kept, and the books and pages read each year.
- Keeps a wishlist of books wanted, which a scan at a bookstore names.
- Records loans: to whom, the loan date, the due date, the return date, with history kept; due dates in a calendar the owner's phone can subscribe to.
- Records courses a book or document was used for: school, level, department and number, title, term, instructor, and whether it was required.
- Catalogs articles, handouts, syllabi, notes, and exams from school, with their files, searchable text, and citations.
- Shares the catalog read-only, without a passkey, with anyone the owner gives a link to: all of it, or the books and papers the owner chooses for that link.
- Prints private barcode labels for the inside cover, and spine labels when the owner turns them on, on the Brother HL-L2460DW.
- Plans the shelves: given each shelf's width, height, and depth, and areas of shelves with the owner's rules for which books go there and in what order, suggests a layout when asked, and once it is put to use says where any book belongs.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Shape | One web app, installable on the phone (PWA). No separate desktop or Android app; a Capacitor wrapper is possible later if the phone's browser falls short. |
| Stack | Go 1.27, SQLite (modernc), goose, sqlc, huma; Vue 3, TypeScript, Vite, Pinia, PrimeVue, Tailwind, Font Awesome Pro. The built frontend is embedded in the binary. Kindred Nights is the reference for tooling and host mechanics. |
| Live updates | REST for every read and write, and beside it one change feed, `GET /api/v1/changes` as Server-Sent Events, for the owner alone (2026-11-05). Triggers write a row to the `changes` table in the transaction that makes each change, so what any process changes is in it: the web server, its background lookup and scans, the text service, an import run by hand. The web server reads the table for each open stream every half second and sends each change on as its type (a book, a scan, a paper, a book's lookup, a loan, a copy's label) and id; a page fetches again what it shows. A new stream starts five seconds back, so nothing made between a page's fetch and its stream opening is lost. A stream that drops goes on from the last change it had, across a restart of the server; one from too long ago (past the newest 10,000 changes) is told to fetch everything. Nothing polls. Kindred Nights is the model, but keeps its feed in memory, which would never hear the text service or the command line. Visitors have no feed. |
| Host | The DigitalOcean droplet: `/opt/homelibrary`, hardened systemd unit, `127.0.0.1:8130`, Apache in front with the existing `jamescwill.com` certificate covering both names. |
| Two names | One Apache VirtualHost with a `ServerAlias`. The app answers on every name in `HL_ORIGINS` and builds nothing from a single base URL. Cookies are per name, and passkeys belong to the name they were made on, so each name gets its own passkey (see Sign-in). |
| Sign-in | Passkeys only, for one owner. The relying party is the name the request came on. The first passkey on a name is made with a one-time setup code: `homelibrary setup-code` on the host, or Settings → Set up another device or address once signed in. The owner keeps passkeys in 1Password, which syncs them, so it is one passkey a name for every device. |
| Sharing | Read-only share links: a secret address the owner makes and names ("Mom", "Book club"), revokes, and may give an end date. Each link shows what the owner chose for it, and the owner may change that at any time without changing its address: every book, those of the categories chosen and the books chosen one by one, or none, with the wanted ones among them or not; every paper, a course's and the papers chosen, or none. A visitor's series and reading are those of the books their link shows, and their pages are only those it has something for. Opening one lets a visitor browse without a passkey: the list, search, covers, a book's details, reading status, and whether a copy is on the shelf or out. Never shown to a visitor: prices, where and when a copy came from, its condition and where in the house it is kept, the owner's notes on a book or a copy, borrowers' names and loan dates, the courses (they say where and when the owner studied, and with whom), the books and papers their link does not show, settings, passkeys. A visitor cannot change anything or start a lookup. The link works on both names; only its hash is kept, so it is shown once, when made. Revoking a link, or its end date passing, turns its visitors away on their next request, to a page that says the link has ended. |
| Barcodes | Each copy's barcode is `HL` and its copy number in five digits (`HL00042`), printed as Code 128. The prefix tells a scan of the owner's label from a book's ISBN. |
| Labels | Barcode labels go inside the front cover, out of the way, on Avery 6467 (1/2" × 1¾", 80 a sheet, removable adhesive; 5167's template, which is permanent and not for books). Spine labels are a separate kind on Avery 5195 (⅔" × 1¾", 60 a sheet, permanent), off until the owner turns them on for a shelving scheme, and their queue keeps filling meanwhile; through the printer's manual feed slot one sheet at a time, media type Label, printed at actual size. Labels queue until a sheet is full and print in shelf order. The server draws the PDFs itself (Courier, which every PDF reader has, and its own Code 128), so nothing is embedded or added. An offset per sheet, in millimetres, moves the printing to meet the printer, and an alignment sheet on plain paper shows by how much. |
| Shelving | Each section has a rule its call numbers follow: by Dewey number (`823.92 MCD`), by author under a prefix (`FIC MCD`), or by series under a prefix (`MANGA ONE v.12`), set on the Labels page. A copy without a section follows the Dewey rule. The owner shelves without sections: the three the library began with, which the Bookshelf import filled from its shelves, were removed (2026-11-04), and the import no longer makes them. |
| Documents | Kept privately: their files live in their own directory, served only through the API, never under `/media`. A visitor sees a document only when their link shows it (as one of its papers, or of a course it shares), without the notes, the course, or the text read from it. The words in its files are read by a separate text service (pdftotext, then Tesseract for photos and scanned pages), the tools at the lowest priority the host has, and searched with SQLite's full-text index. A DOI fills a document's blanks from Crossref. Photos of pages go through the cover photo tool, straightened, at 2400 pixels for small print. |
| Citations | APA 7, MLA 9, and Chicago (bibliography) for every book and document, copied with their italics. |
| Shelf planner | The owner measures each shelf: width inside the sides, clear height, depth, and the corner braces many shelves have under the shelf above (how far each hangs down and how far it reaches in from the side). A new bookcase's shelves share one set of measurements but for the heights of any that differ, such as a taller bottom shelf, and each shelf can be changed after. Shelves are grouped into **areas** (2026-11-13), each with rules for which books it takes (categories, a Dewey range, words in a subject heading, authors, series, a height at least or at most) and an order (subject, author, series and volume, title, date, height, in the owner's sequence). Subject is the Dewey number, or one estimated from the LC class where a book has none, an estimate shown as such and never stored. A book goes to the first area, in the owner's order, whose rules take it; an area with no rules takes the rest. An area's shelves need not stand together (the bottom shelf of every bookcase can be the tall books' area), and it fills them in walking order, each to a target (80% unless set) for books to come; a book too tall for the shelf its turn comes on is set aside, for an area of tall books, tried first, to take. No book is given a place until the owner asks for a suggestion, and none has one until a suggestion is put to use. Only a book short enough to clear a brace stands at either end of a braced shelf: a tall first book starts past the brace, and the shelf says how much to leave empty, and tall books stop short of the far one. Put to use, each shelf keeps where it starts in its area's order, so a new book goes to the shelf of its area it belongs on, into the room left; a shelf whose area or area's order changes since has no place in the layout until the next suggestion is put to use. A book's size is measured where a source gave it, and worked out otherwise from its format and pages. Shelf-edge range labels print on the spine-label sheet. Where a copy stands is the owner's alone. |
| Measurements | Lengths are kept in whole millimetres, as most of them arrive (208 of the 239 book heights came from the Library of Congress in centimetres). The owner reads and types them in inches or centimetres, one choice for every measurement in the app (shelves, braces, a book's size), kept on each device. A length shows as the plainest number that comes back to the same millimetres: whole, half, and quarter inches first, read to a tenth and typed to a hundredth, so 11 in stays 11 and never becomes 10.98. |
| Data sources | Free sources only, by the owner's decision: Library of Congress SRU (MARC), HathiTrust's Bibliographic API (the research libraries' MARC, CC0), Open Library, Google Books (key restricted to the Books API and the droplet's address), Hardcover (the owner's free API token, on the droplet only), the Library of Congress Subject Headings to check Open Library's subjects, Crossref for DOIs, and OpenAlex for what a DOI is about. No paid API and no scraping (ISBNdb was weighed on 2026-11-05 and left: its terms have its data deleted within 30 days of a subscription ending, and forbid building a database from it); a gap is filled by hand on the book's Edit page or by the owner's photo. Descriptions arrive as plain text: markup from any source, and the Bookshelf export's `&&L`/`&&R`, is turned into paragraphs. |

## Data sources and how they merge

Every lookup asks the sources at once, keeps each raw answer in `lookups`, and merges field by field. A field keeps the source it came from in `editions.sources`, so a later lookup fills blanks and never overwrites what the owner typed. A lookup the owner asks for, of one book or of every book in the background, also keeps what the sources proposed and offers what they give otherwise, for the owner to take or leave each; the same words in another case, spacing, or punctuation, and a date the book has more exactly, are no difference. What is offered waits on the Suggested changes page until the owner decides, and a value the owner keeps the book's own against is not offered again unless a source gives something else.

| Field | First choice, then the next |
| --- | --- |
| Title, subtitle | Google, Open Library, LoC |
| Contributors | Google, Open Library, then LoC's 100/700 with roles from `$e` (translator, illustrator, editor) |
| Publisher, date | Google, Open Library, LoC 264/260 |
| Pages | Open Library, Google, LoC 300 `$a` |
| Format | Open Library `physical_format`, LoC 020 `$q`; "paperback" is refined to mass market or trade when a source says which |
| Height | LoC 300 `$c`, HathiTrust 300 `$c`, Open Library dimensions, Google's volume record |
| Width, thickness | Open Library dimensions, LoC, Google's volume record (a search answer has no measurements, so Google's lookup reads the whole record of the volume that carries the ISBN) |
| Dewey | LoC 082 for this ISBN, HathiTrust 082, Open Library, LoC 082 for another edition found by title and author, then the series' own number |
| LC classification | LoC 050, HathiTrust 050, Open Library |
| Series | Open Library, LoC, Google, then Hardcover; a name in two scripts keeps its English half. A number written into the name ("-- bk. 9", ": Book 2", "Book six of the …") is read out of it. A series is matched to the library's own by its name without case, punctuation, or an article at either end, and takes the library's spelling; a series the owner said is none is never filled or offered |
| Subjects | LoC 650/600/651 (HathiTrust's where the LoC has none) and Google categories together; Open Library's only when neither has any, and then only those that are Library of Congress Subject Headings |
| Description | Google, Open Library |
| Cover | Open Library's every picture of the edition as uploaded (its "large" is cut to 500 pixels high), then Hardcover's for the edition or the book, or the owner's photo. Google's image host refuses the droplet (403), so Google gives text only; its thumbnails are shown in search results, never kept |

Measured on 2026-11-04 against 30 of the owner's books: LoC and Open Library gave a Dewey number for 18. The misses were paperback reprints (LoC catalogs the hardcover, which the title-and-author search finds) and the newest manga volumes (which take the series' number). LoC also answers `[Fic]` and `B`, which are not class numbers and are dropped.

## Data model

Work, edition, copy, as a library keeps them.

- **Work**: the book in the abstract. Title, series and number, category (fiction, nonfiction, manga, light novel, graphic novel, or uncategorized: set by the owner or an import, or else worked out whenever the book is read from its class number, subjects, series, and publisher), read status and progress, rating, notes, genres. Read status lives here, so a second copy or edition does not reset it.
- **Edition**: one ISBN. Everything bibliographic, the cover, and the field sources.
- **Copy**: the physical book. Barcode (from its number), section, new or used, acquired date, source, and price, condition, dust jacket, signed, location, notes. A copy that has gone (lost, sold, donated, given away, discarded) keeps its record with the day and a note; it leaves the shelves, the labels, and the counts, and can come back. A shelf check records the day each copy was last seen.
- **People** and **series** are shared by name. **Sections** and **genres** are the owner's own lists; the library has no sections now (see Shelving).
- **Lookups** keep raw answers from each source, **lookup proposals** what the sources proposed for an edition at the last lookup the owner asked for, and **declined suggestions** the values the owner kept the book's own against, so what is offered is worked out from them and the book as it is; **enrichment** is the queue of editions waiting for a lookup, and **import refs** tie a copy to the record it came from in another app, with that record's web address where it has one, so an import can run twice and a book's page links back. **Document import refs** do the same for a document an EndNote export brought.
- **Borrowers** are shared by name and come into being with their first loan. A **loan** is one copy lent once: lent on, due back (optional), returned on, notes. A loan with no return date is a copy still out; a copy is with one borrower at a time, and returned loans stay as the history. A copy that is out cannot be removed, nor its book, until it is back. Overdue is counted on the owner's device, against the day there.
- **Courses**: school, level (high school, undergraduate, graduate, professional development, other), department and number, title, term as a season and a year so courses sort as they were taken, instructor, notes. A course lists **copies**, each required or not: the physical book, since that is the one with the notes in it.

- **Call numbers** are worked out, not kept: the class (the Dewey number, or the section's prefix), a three-letter Cutter (the author's surname, or the series under series shelving, or the title past its article when there is no author), and `v.` with the series number. Shelf order is section, class (numbers as numbers), Cutter, then an author's series together in volume order, then title.
- An edition the owner wants and has no copy of is on the **wishlist**; its first copy takes it off.
- **Readings** keep each earlier read of a work, started and finished; the work holds the read in progress or the latest.
- A **share link** keeps its name, its token's hash, its end, and what it shows: its books (every one, some, or none), whether the wanted ones show, its papers (every one, some, or none), and for "some", the categories, books, courses, and papers chosen. A book, course, or paper removed leaves every link.
- A **calendar link** is a secret address, kept as its hash like a share link, for a feed of the loans' due dates.
- The **label queue** holds a barcode and a spine label for every copy until printed; a new copy joins it, and the owner can put one back to print again. **Label offsets** are kept per sheet.

- **Documents**, called papers in the app (2026-11-05; the API and the code keep "documents"): kind (article, chapter, book review, handout, syllabus, notes, exam, other), title, authors as written, a chapter's book's editors, where it appeared (journal or book), volume, issue, pages, date, publisher, DOI, link, subjects, an abstract (which a visitor sees, as the subjects), a course, notes, and whether visitors may see it. **Document files** are PDFs or page photos in order, each with the text read from it, or typed by the owner, which the text service then leaves alone. A full-text index holds each document's details, its subjects, its abstract, and its files' text.

- **Bookcases** in walking order, each with **shelves** from the top down: width, height, depth, and corner brace height and width (both or neither) in millimetres, the area it is in, and, while a layout is in use, where the shelf starts in its area's order (the area, the order then, and the first book's place in it). **Areas** in the order a book tries them, each with its rules (categories, a Dewey range, subject words, authors, series, heights) and its order of keys. **Layout settings** hold the fill target and when the layout in use was put to use.

## Phases

1. **Catalog** — the data model; add by ISBN, title search, or by hand; the merged lookup with a review form; covers fetched or photographed; the library list with search and filters; a book's page; read status and acquisition details; the Bookshelf import with background enrichment; passkey sign-in on both names; deploy.
2. **Scanning** — the phone camera reads ISBN barcodes (the browser's own BarcodeDetector, zxing-wasm where there is none), with the torch where the phone has one; a Scan page where every barcode joins one list as it is scanned: each ISBN is queued and looked up in the background, then shown as already owned, found (with what the sources say, waiting for the owner), or unknown to every source, with a running count beside the camera; the owner adds the found books one at a time or all at once, choosing their section and whether they were bought new or used, and they are added in the background (nothing is added without the owner's word, so the same page answers "do I own this?" at a bookstore); a Scan button on Add a book; mass-market UPCs explain where the ISBN is printed; the owner's own `HL` labels open their book. Automatic cover detection: OpenCV.js finds the book in a photo and places the four corners. A service worker and a manifest id, so browsers that install web apps offer to.
3. **Sharing** — read-only share links (see Decisions): made, named, given an end date, given what each shows (books by category or one by one, the wishlist, papers by course or one by one) and changed later (2026-11-05), and revoked in Settings; a visitor's view of the list and a book's page with the owner-only fields left out by the server, not hidden by the page; visitors limited to reading, rate-limited, and never indexed. Each later phase says what a visitor sees of what it adds.
4. **Loans and courses** — lend a copy from its book's page or from Loans, by choosing the book; mark it returned; a Loans page with what is out (overdue first, and said in words), the borrowers, and the history; courses with their books, put on a course from either side. Navigation has six places (Library, Scan, Add, Loans, Courses, Settings), and Import starts from Add. A visitor sees whether each copy is on the shelf or out, and nothing of borrowers, loan dates, or courses.
5. **Labels** — call numbers from each section's shelving rule, on every copy and visible to visitors; the label queue in shelf order, with what a call number lacks (a Dewey number, a volume) flagged before spine labels print; PDFs for Avery 5167 and 5195 opened in the browser to print, with an offset per sheet and an alignment sheet; a Labels button on the library that says when sheets are ready; scanning the owner's own label opens the copy, marked, and picks it when lending.
6. **Documents** — a Documents tab beside Books in the Library; add a document by hand or from a DOI (Crossref), then its PDF or photos of its pages; the text service reads them in the background; search by any word in a document or its files, with the match shown; citations for documents and books; a course's documents on its page. A visitor sees only the documents their link shows. The library search also covers subjects, genres, everyone credited, and the LC class.
7. **Shelf planner** — a Shelves page (from the Library) with bookcases and shelves, their corner braces, a new bookcase's shelves given their own heights where they differ (2026-11-13), measured in inches or centimetres as every length in the app is; areas of shelves with the owner's rules for which books they take and the order they stand in, the Dewey number estimated from the LC class where a book has none, a layout suggested only when asked and filled to a target with room to grow, put to use when the books are on the shelves (2026-11-13, in place of a layout proposed unasked in call-number order and oversize shelves); a map of every shelf with its range and how full it is, with what does not fit or is too tall; shelf-edge range labels; "where does this go?" by search or by scanning a label, and each copy's shelf on its book's page.
8. **Upkeep** — the library exported from Settings, as a CSV a row a copy and as one JSON file of everything; the nightly backup keeps the covers and documents' files beside the database, and the owner's PC copies the newest backup home each day into OneDrive; one Import for Bookshelf, Goodreads, and LibraryThing exports, which adds the books the owner has, links the ones already here to their Goodreads or LibraryThing page, and fills in reading they lack; Hardcover as a source for the covers Open Library lacks (46 of the 84 books without one) and for a series no other source names.
9. **Upkeep, continued** — a wishlist (added by hand, from a scan, or from Goodreads' to-read shelf and LibraryThing's wish list) that a scan names; copies that have gone, kept with the day, and a shelf check that scans a shelf and says what is missing or misplaced; a Series page with each series' volumes, the gaps, and the next number, where a series is renamed or merged; earlier reads kept and a Reading page with what is being read and the books and pages finished each year; a page of the details books lack, to fill by hand or look up again; a calendar feed of loans' due dates; the Scan page answering "do I own this?" without a connection, from a list the installed app keeps; barcode labels for the inside cover on removable stock, spine labels off until wanted; known-vulnerability scans of the Go and npm dependencies in lint and CI; end-to-end tests in a real browser. A visitor sees the wishlist, the Series and Reading pages, and earlier reads; nothing of copies that have gone, shelf checks, missing details, or the calendar.
10. **Corrections** — every cover a source has, at its full size, named by its source and its size in pixels, Hardcover's shown as Open Library's are; "Look up again" on a book filling its blanks and offering each detail the sources now give otherwise, to take or leave one by one; the reading status changed from the library list, and a whole series marked read; the library list keeping its page and order when the owner comes back from a book, Back never walking through an edit again, and a breadcrumb trail over the deeper pages; a book marked read given no finish day the owner did not enter; a choice in Settings of matching the device, light, or dark, kept on the device; each series on a page of its own, with its books in order, where a book is taken out of the series, the series is renamed or merged, marked read, said to be no series (a publisher's line), or looked up again book by book with every suggested change offered together; "Look up again" asking each source afresh rather than from the answers kept; a field the owner emptied staying empty through every lookup; every book looked up again in the background from the Missing details page, each source asked afresh, to fill whatever blanks the sources can now fill, a book added meanwhile looked up first (2026-11-05), and what the sources give otherwise kept for a page of suggested changes, a book or many at a time, where every detail is taken or kept only as the owner chooses, one left undecided waiting there, a detail kept not offered again unless a source gives something else, and the same words in another case or punctuation, or a date given less exactly, no change at all (2026-11-05); a paper's DOI found by searching Crossref for its title and first author, the one chosen filling the form's blanks as a DOI typed does (2026-11-05); a paper with a DOI looked up again from its page, or every one from the Papers list, its blanks filled (the abstract, the kind, the subjects among them) and what Crossref gives otherwise offered to take or leave, as a book's are; a book review a kind of paper; the Papers list sorted by its title, kind, year, course, or files (2026-11-05); a document's Find a copy: the open copy OpenAlex knows (an author's manuscript in a repository, or the publisher's open version), named with its version and licence and added to the files when the owner says, a search of the owner's library (its EBSCO databases, set in Settings and kept on the device) where they sign in with their own card, and the publisher's page; a citation exported from the library's database as RIS filling the document form's blanks, as a DOI does, with its subject terms and abstract; an abstract of a document's own, filled from Crossref, OpenAlex, an export, or the EndNote import, and shown on its page; a chapter's book's editors, from Crossref, an export, or the EndNote import, named in its citation in each style; documents taking their PDFs as they are added, and subjects, which a DOI brings from OpenAlex and Crossref and the search covers. A visitor sees a document's subjects, as a book's. EndNote's XML export imported: its books join the library as the other apps' do, its articles, chapters, and other papers become documents, and for every app the owner chooses where books not marked as owned go (left out, the shelves, or the wishlist); a row imported before, or a book or paper already here, gains what the file has and it lacks (its blank details, as a lookup fills them, and its reading), nothing it has replaced, so a file imported again fills what is blank once more; one the house has in another edition (the same title before any subtitle and the same first author's surname) is the owner's, matched and never wished for, and a wanted book so had says which edition the house has, and comes off the wishlist with the others like it in one step, after asking.
